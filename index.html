About

Hi, I'm Areeg.

I am a PhD candidate in computer science at the University of Minnesota, advised by Mohamed Mokbel, and a GAGE Fellow for Spring 2027. I work in data management and data systems.

I build data systems that give accurate answers even when the data falls short. The only trip data cities still publish is coarse, zone-to-zone summaries, and no method could turn those into accurate trip times. My system CoarseETA does. It cuts error from 55.8% to 16.4% in San Francisco, lands within a few points of a model trained on full trip-level data, and adds only about 1 ms per query.

I also lead a team of eight undergraduate researchers building ETAlon, a benchmark that puts competing methods on equal footing. My dissertation work won first place at the ACM SIGSPATIAL 2026 Student Research Competition.

My earlier work appears under the name Arig Mostafa.

Research

The services people rely on every day, travel time estimates, routing, delivery windows, all depend on trip data held by a handful of companies. My current projects build the methods and systems that make those services possible from data anyone can get.

CoarseETA

ACM SIGSPATIAL 2026

Answers per-trip travel time queries from the coarse, zone-level data that cities still publish, by pairing those aggregates with an open-source routing engine's ranking of trips. Precomputed rankings sit in a hash index over zone pairs with a B-tree per pair, so it adds about 1 ms per query.

  • Query processing
  • Indexing
Preprint GitHub

ETAlon

In progress

A reusable benchmark system that runs every method on the same test trips, grouped by the data each one needs. It scores 8 methods on about 735K trips so far, built by the 8 undergraduate researchers I lead.

  • Benchmarking
  • Evaluation
Website

ResETA

In progress

A calibration layer that sharpens any travel time method, treating it as a black box. The key insight: going coarser does not just lose information, it also gains context. Zone-level statistics are dense where trip-level data is sparse, so the layer learns its corrections from both.

  • Learned calibration
  • Residual learning

TrajSplit

IEEE MDM 2025

Cleans raw GPS streams into single-destination trips with a principled trip definition, plus two heuristics that keep it scalable at above 95% of exhaustive accuracy. Trip-duration error fell from about 150% to under 30%.

  • Data cleaning
  • Scalability
Paper GitHub

Mapedia

SIGSPATIAL 2023 vision

A scalable system for inferring and managing the road metadata OpenStreetMap lacks, with four inference modules over a metadata store.

  • Data inference
  • Metadata management
Vision paper Website

Road Network Genome

SIGSPATIAL 2026 vision

With Mohamed Hemdan, a vision for embedding a whole road network as one vector so regions can share models and data. It calls for multi-scale storage and similarity search over vectors with millions of dimensions.

  • Representation learning
  • Vector search

A Grand Tour of ETA Queries

VLDB 2026 tutorial

A survey of 67 papers that rates five families of methods on accuracy, latency, precomputation, and reliance on historical data, and traces the systems behind Google Maps, DiDi, Uber, and Lyft.

  • Survey
  • Cost tradeoffs
